About these numbers

  1. Day-to-day spending is what school systems reported spending in 2023-24 on instruction, support, administration, buildings, buses and food (functions 1000-3999 in the state's chart of accounts). It leaves out construction, debt payments and money passed from one school system to another, such as Orleans Parish School Board's payments to New Orleans charter schools, so nothing is counted twice.
  2. Teacher pay and administrator pay are salary dollars only (object codes 112 and 111), not benefits. "Administrators" means everyone the state codes as an official or administrator: superintendents, principals, assistant principals, charter school chief executives, supervisors and directors.
  3. Per student uses the October 2023 count of students at each school. Per resident uses the Census Bureau's July 2023 parish population estimate [3]. Per-resident figures are context, not a measure of local taxes: much of the money is state and federal.
  4. Charter schools that are their own school systems are counted in the parish where their school sits. Charters run under a parish school board (Types 1, 3 and 4) are already inside that board's figures.
  5. The long-run chart uses the Census Bureau's Annual Survey of School System Finances [4], adjusted to 2024 dollars with the consumer price index [5]. Census does not count most charter schools, so it covers parish and city systems only, and New Orleans stops at 2005: after Hurricane Katrina most of its schools became charters.
  6. The state and Census figures agree closely. For each of the 70 parish and city systems, the state's 2023-24 day-to-day spending is within 5% of Census's figure for the same year, usually within about 1%.

Sources

  1. Louisiana Department of Education, 2023-2024 Annual Financial Report data, Report 2: Expenditures (financial data page).
  2. Louisiana Department of Education, Multiple Statistics by Site, October 2, 2023.
  3. U.S. Census Bureau, county population estimates, 2020-2024.
  4. U.S. Census Bureau, Annual Survey of School System Finances, fiscal years 1992-2024 (1999 not posted).
  5. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, via FRED, averaged over each July-June school year.
  6. Each school system's website; see the links in the table above. Audited financial statements for every school board and most charters are also posted by the Louisiana Legislative Auditor.
