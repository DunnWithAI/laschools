About these numbers

  1. Day-to-day spending is what school systems reported spending each year on instruction, support, administration, buildings, buses and food (functions 1000-3999 in the state's chart of accounts). It leaves out construction, debt payments and money passed from one school system to another, such as Orleans Parish School Board's payments to New Orleans charter schools, so nothing is counted twice.
  2. Dollars. Figures for a single year are in that year's dollars. Anything that compares years ("since 2008-09", the over-time charts) is adjusted to 2024-25 dollars with the consumer price index [5].
  3. Teacher pay and administrator pay are salary dollars only (object codes 112 and 111), not benefits. "Administrators" means everyone the state codes as an official or administrator: superintendents, principals, assistant principals, charter school chief executives, supervisors and directors. The 2024-25 file leaves out these codes, so they are filled in from the same line items in earlier years, where every line item kept the same code.
  4. Per student uses each October's count of students at each school [2]. Per resident uses the Census Bureau's July population estimate for the parish [3]. Per-resident figures are context, not a measure of local taxes: much of the money is state and federal.
  5. Charter schools that are their own school systems are counted in the parish where their school sits, found through the federal school directory [7]. Charters run under a parish school board (Types 1, 3 and 4) are inside that board's figures. A few state-run schools (for example the schools for the deaf and blind) report no spending in this data, so their students, under 1,600 a year, are left out.
  6. The longer view uses the Census Bureau's Annual Survey of School System Finances [4]. Census does not count most charter schools, so it covers parish and city systems only, and New Orleans stops at 2005: after Hurricane Katrina most of its schools became charters. Census figures for 2011-12 run 15% to 28% above the state's for about ten systems and fall back the next year; the state's figures do not jump.
  7. Apart from that year, the two sources agree. For every parish and city system in every year from 2008-09 to 2023-24, the state's day-to-day spending is within 11% of Census's figure, and usually within about 1%.

Sources

  1. Louisiana Department of Education, Annual Financial Report data, Report 2: Expenditures, 2008-09 to 2024-25, from the financial data page (for example 2023-24).
  2. Louisiana Department of Education, Multiple Statistics by Site, each October 2008 to 2024, from the enrollment data page.
  3. U.S. Census Bureau, county population estimates: 2000-2010, 2010-2020, 2020-2024.
  4. U.S. Census Bureau, Annual Survey of School System Finances, fiscal years 1992-2024 (1999 not posted).
  5. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, via FRED, averaged over each July-June school year.
  6. Each school system's website; see the links in the table above. Audited financial statements for every school board and most charters are also posted by the Louisiana Legislative Auditor.
  7. National Center for Education Statistics, Common Core of Data school directory, 2008-2024, via the Urban Institute Education Data Portal.

Figures are calculated, not copied from a published table; check the sources before quoting them.
